3b · Every state of the window

The overview strip, drawn alone at the plot's width. A ring is the pointer: hollow while it hovers, filled while the button is held; the tag beside it is the cursor. The vertical line is the playhead, which the strip always shows, in or out of the window.

1At rest

The window is always there. Zoomed out it spans the track and nothing is dimmed; zoomed, the rest of the track sits under a scrim. Its handles are 6 px bars centered on its edges, the body a 7% fill between two hairlines.

whole track
bars 17–24

2Hover

Over the body the fill lifts to 12% and the cursor is grab. Over a handle the bar goes to full --text and the cursor is ew-resize. A handle's target is 16 px: 8 either side of the edge, and it wins over the body where they overlap. The outer 8 px exist at the track's ends too, past the plot.

3Press

On pointerdown the strip captures the pointer and remembers where on the body or handle it was grabbed, so nothing jumps under it. The pressed look comes at once (17% fill, full-strength lines; a handle gains a 2 px halo) and the cursor locks to grabbing or ew-resize for the whole page. Nothing moves until the pointer has travelled 3 px: a press and release inside that is a click, changes nothing and reports nothing, which is what lets a double-click through.

4Dragging the body

The window keeps its width and steps bar by bar with the pointer; a tag above it names the bars it holds. Every row below redraws at each step, in the browser, and the head line's readout follows. The pointer may leave the strip in any direction: only its x counts.

5The body against the track's end

The window stops at the end and keeps its width; the pointer carries on alone. It picks the window up again only when it comes back to where it grabbed it.

8A handle against the track's edge

It stops at the first sample (or the last) and the pointer goes on without it. The first and last snaps are the file's own ends, which rarely fall on a bar line.

9Release, and a drag that is called off

On pointerup the tag goes, the window rests where it is (hovered, since the pointer is still on it) and the page reports the new bars to the server, once. A capture lost without a release counts as a release. Esc during a drag, or pointercancel, puts the window back where the press found it and reports nothing.

10Double-click

Zoomed, it goes to the whole track and remembers the zoom; on the whole track, it goes back to that zoom. With no zoom to go back to (the page just opened) it takes 8 bars around the place clicked. Enter on the focused window does the same.

11Pressing the strip outside the window

As built today: the window comes to the pointer, centered on it and snapped, and the same press carries on as a drag of the body. Zoomed out there is no outside.

12Playing: the playhead crosses the window's edge

Follow is not a control. While playing, when the playhead reaches the window's right edge the window moves on by half its width (a whole number of bars, at least one), in one step with no slide, so the playhead is then at its middle. At the track's end the window stops against it. Zoomed out there is nothing to follow.

13Playing: a drag, and a view left behind

The audio never notices a drag. From press to release the window does not follow. If the release leaves the playhead outside the window, the view stays where it was put: the playhead runs on in the strip, the plot shows none, and the head line offers Go to the playhead · bar 31. Following starts again when the playhead is next inside the window, by that button, by a seek, or by playing into it. ‹ › behave the same way.

14Loop on

The window's top line thickens to 3 px at full strength: the window is the loop. The playhead returns to its left edge on reaching its right one, and the view does not follow. Switched on with the playhead outside the window, playback moves to the window's start at once. Live's own loop brace on the ruler is unchanged and is not this.

15Dragging while it loops

Until the release the audio keeps looping the bars it had, drawn as a dashed ghost, while the window shows where the loop will be. On release the loop is the window; if the playhead is then outside it, playback moves to its start. Widening the window to the whole track switches loop off.

16Without a warp, and from the keyboard

With no warp the edges snap to whole seconds and the tag gives times. Each handle is a focusable slider (←/→ one bar, Shift four, Home/End the track's ends) and the body a focusable button (←/→ move it a bar, Enter as a double-click); a move from the keyboard reports when the key is released.

3c · Seeking: ruler, lane, key strip, drawer

1The ruler seeks to the start of the bar

Over the ruler the cursor is the seek cursor, a stem with a play triangle (16 px, hotspot on the stem), and a faint line (1 px --text-30) runs from the ruler to the key strip at the start of the bar under the pointer: where playback will land. A click puts the playhead there; playing carries on from it, paused stays paused. Here the pointer is inside bar 20, so the line is on bar 20's first beat while the playhead plays on in bar 19.

5What does not seek

The overview strip moves the view and never the playhead. The tempo line, Off the grid, the chord placeholder, the time axis and the gutters do nothing. Outside the warp view only the player's own slim waveform seeks; it seeks to the exact spot, and a spot outside the window brings the window to the bar it is in.
