Track details around the judge's text, a key strip and sharing

The evidence panel goes back to two even columns. On the left is the argument: Why this key, Considered, Human sources and a folded First pass: withheld. On the right is the data: the detectors, the Tonic check, the folded progression and Tempo. Windows leave the panel. Each one opens from its band on the timeline, in a drawer under the warp view. A key strip becomes the warp view's last lane and replaces Key flicker, with the chord lane and a playhead given room below it. Share sits at the head of the evidence panel and draws its own 1200 px card.

The detector readings, windows and recorded text for the two named tracks come from the brief's screenshots. The new-style texts are mine, rewritten to ADR 0064's budgets from the judge's own arguments, so they are not the judge's words. The withheld track, the song with sections, Takyon's key flicker after its tenth change and Starry Eyed's six changes are all invented.

Left is the argument, right is the evidence. Without the windows, the right column is three short blocks plus Tempo, and the left is about 100 words plus a short list. Both end near the same height on a new verdict (Starry Eyed, Takyon). An old verdict still runs long on the left, but nothing on the right grows to match it, and nothing stacks there any more.

Considered sits under Why this key, with each key in mono in a 92 px column and its sentence beside it. A reader asks "why not G♯ minor?" after reading the why, so it goes there, not by the detectors. A WITHHELD heads it Still open, because those keys are not rejected. An old run with no candidates draws no list.

First pass: withheld folds at the foot of the left column with its date. It is drawn once, from the run, never per window. Open, it is quieter than the verdict's own text (13 px, --text-60) because it is superseded reasoning.

The detector row is one line at 494 px: Detector 84 · Key 88 · Score 50 · Band 70 · Runner-up. Column heads replace the inline runner-up word, which would cost a column of width on every row. The heads say Score, not r, because madmom's figure is not an r. The band chip names the band only. Its full text goes in the tooltip: Unreliable band, r below 0.70: distrust. I also gave the other bands a chip (r > 0.75, 0.70–0.75), because watch for a relative-key swap was useful on Starry Eyed. None is colored: a band is a fact, not a state.

The progression folds its loops, with the count (×18) at --text-50 and the separators at --text-30, so the runs read first. Show all 70 opens the plain list, which is what the judge saw.

Typesetting is ADR 0064's: keys, chords and figures in Plex Mono at .92 em so they sit on the Sans x-height, and MEDIUM in small caps with no color. Prose spells keys in full (E major), and headings follow: Why E major, not today's Why E maj. The band keeps E maj.

Share is the panel's one button, at the head of what it shares. The verdict band stays four facts. Its Why E maj ↓ link already brings you here, and the card holds this panel's content, not the band's. The menu says what each action makes. Afterwards the button reads ✓ Card copied for a few seconds, in neutral ink: it is an acknowledgment, not done in the green sense.

A window opens from its tag. The tag on the ruler is a button. Clicking it fills the tag, strengthens its band in every row, and opens one drawer under the timeline, with a caret at the band's center. The drawer holds the window's label and span, its one-sentence why, what it became (→ section Chorus: E minor or → no section) and its readings in the panel's detector row. ‹ › step through the windows and × or Esc closes it. Only one is open at a time, so the page never grows by three tables.

Under the timeline, not beside it. The warp view already spends the full column width, and a drawer beside it would squeeze the plot. A drawer under it keeps the plot's 832 px and lines the caret up with the band. The selected lane's detail (file, seven facts, the two controls) still follows below, unchanged.

The key strip is the last row before the time axis, 52 px with a slightly stronger top rule, because it describes the song rather than a stem. Its bands are the verdict's centers: outlined boxes with the short key at --text. A single center is one band across the track. The raw running estimate is a 17 px underlay along the strip's foot. Each change is a block shaded by its r (alpha r × 0.22, at most .18), with a 3 px tick where it starts. A change is labeled only where its block is 40 px or wider, at 9.5 px --text-40. Its title gives the rest: 0:03.39 · E min · r 0.36. So zoomed out it is texture, and at 8 bars it reads change by change, for the judge's eye.

A section is placed only where its time is known. The ledger's Section has a label and a center but no times (ADR 0064 leaves timestamps undecided). A section that came from a window takes the window's span. The primary center fills everything else. A section with no time is not drawn: the gutter names it (bridge · C Lyd: no time given). That is the invented Sections state. Without this rule the strip would invent placements.

Takyon at whole track shows why the strip earns its place. One band says B minor, while the faint underlay reads E minor from 0:21 to 1:57. That is the same tension Why this key argues, now visible on the timeline.

Room for what comes next: the chord lane goes under the key strip, the same width, its blocks labeled like the key bands. The transport takes the ruler's gutter, the one empty cell at the top left: a 28 px play button and 0:47.20 / 2:48, with Bar moving into the tick labels. The playhead is a 1 px --text line with a head on the ruler, crossing every row. It is not teal, because playing is not the machine working. The zoom pills stay where they are.

Its own layout, top-weighted. The first 630 px (a link-preview ratio) carry everything a friend scans: Artist – Title, then key, confidence and BPM in the band's three cells and short forms, then the subtitle and the three readings. The prose sits below, for whoever opens the image.

Sizes come from the preview, not the page. At 36% the 76 px key is 27 px and the 21 px prose is under 8 px. So the figures read in the preview, and the prose reads once the image is opened, which is how a picture in a chat gets read. I don't know Discord's exact preview box for a tall image, and it may be smaller. One real paste settles it, and the type scale is the only lever.

The app's dark palette, for three reasons. The card is a picture of the verdict as crateside shows it, and a friend who later sees the app should recognize it. The color rule (color means state) needs no second palette. And Discord's dark theme, the common case, puts it on near-black, where a light card would glare. On a light chat theme it reads as a dark image, like any screenshot. Nothing on it is colored except the mark's amber.

Prose takes two columns when there is a tonic check (Why this key left, Tonic check right), and Considered runs underneath as three cells, key over sentence. With no tonic check, Why this key takes the width. The stage flow, sample list, links and the backer line's detail are left off: they mean nothing outside the app.

A verdict with sections adds the key strip under the subtitle, scaled up. Placed sections are drawn and unplaced ones are named in a line under it, by the same rule as 1b.

An old long reasoning is cut, not shrunk. The brief says to cut at a paragraph, but every old reasoning is one paragraph (ADR 0064 measured no newlines). So the cut falls at the last sentence ending within 110 words, followed by …. The type size never changes to fit.
