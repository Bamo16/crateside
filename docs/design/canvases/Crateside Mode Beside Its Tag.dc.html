A verdict's mode, shown beside the tag Ableton gets

The tag stays where it is, and the mode comes before it. Every place that shows a key keeps the tag in the same spot at the same size, so the 96% of plain major and minor songs look exactly as they do now, and a tagged sample and its verdict still read alike. Where the verdict is modal, the mode sits beside the tag. In the dense Tracks row it is a small outlined abbreviation after the tag, with the full sentence on hover. Where there is room it is the arrow, A Dorian → A min, read top to bottom into the large figure, with the reason written underneath. No colour: an outline and the arrow do the work.

Songs in italics aren't real. Figures and sources are made up.

Ionian and Aeolian get no mark. They are major and minor. The three modes the ledger stored as major become Ionian and look as they do now.

Centres on the track's time axis, where the sections have times (windows the maintainer gave, brief 18). The primary is filled, and each other centre is outlined. A repeated section is drawn each time it occurs. Under the strip there is one line per centre with its label, its arrow and what it is, and the primary says the tag follows it.

Without times (a source wrote "chorus only"), the strip isn't drawn and the list stands on its own. Nothing is placed on the axis that wasn't timed.

Each source says what it covered, as a neutral chip under its name: whole song, chorus only. Its ✓ or ✗ is judged against that section, so a chorus-only E minor is a ✓ agrees with the chorus and not a dissent. A source that disagrees with a section says which one. A whole-song reading that matches only a section says so and stays muted.

A stated mode is shown in full, G Mixolydian, and no longer as G major (mixolydian). The source said the mode, so the mode is shown first.

1dPartners: the same-notes key as a third basis pillproposal; not asked of this issue
Working from A min (8A), the tag of A Dorian. Its samples are tagged this way, so it's what Live will match on.
A min C maj relative G maj same notes

Worth building, small. A Dorian uses G major's seven notes, so G-major material sits on it without a clash, which the tag alone can't tell Partners. Offer the same-notes key as a third pill beside the relative, only for a modal verdict. Picking it re-sorts the table from that basis, as the relative pill does now, and the wheel rings the track's own tag. Plain verdicts get no third pill, since their same-notes key is the tag or its relative.
