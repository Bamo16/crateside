Typing first. The range field takes 33–48, 33-48 or 33 48. Tab moves to the label and then the note, and Enter on the last field adds a line. The band draws as you type. Dragging on the ruler adds a line with the range filled in, snapped to whole bars, and its edges can be dragged afterwards. Both edit the same list.

Bands by outline and fill, not hue. Yours are solid with a faint fill and a numbered tag on the ruler. The judge's are dashed with no fill, tagged judge. While the detectors read a window, it alone turns teal (the machine is working), and it goes back to neutral once read. After recording, each tag carries its section's key.

The cost, once and plainly, as the status line above the button: what runs, the tokens, the share of the week. It isn't a confirm step, because the button already says what happens and it isn't destructive. When the 85% guard holds, one sentence is added and the run is still offered, as Judge this one anyway is.

The form's label is amber while it's open, as the stack's editors are. The lanes stay live under it, since the bands are the point.

No warp: the same form, with ranges in m:ss. The head of the form says why, and the ruler counts time. Dragging still works on the time ruler.

Withheld again keeps the form's ranges, so a second try is one edit away. A run that ends without a verdict is resumed once without asking, and the line says so.

A block per window, not a line. Each window has detector readings of its own, and those readings are the evidence. The block repeats the detector table's shape from the whole-file panel, so they read alike.

Whose window it was uses the same outline language as the bands: yours solid, judge dashed, with no colour. A judge's block says that it asked, when, and its reason, and that crateside ran it unattended. You didn't ask for it, so this is where you find out it happened.

The direction and the ear head the section, recorded with the run they went with. The whole-file reading folds under the windows, since it's what the windows were run to get past.

Each block ends with the section it became, which is what brief 17's centres strip draws on the time axis.
