Brief 13 · turn 2

How much a drifting tempo drifts

A drifting tempo now says how much it drifts, in two forms. The range: whole numbers rounded outward, never hundredths. The spread: that range as a percentage of the figure shown, so drifts at different tempos can be compared. Both appear in the note and the tooltip. The song page adds a tempo-over-time strip showing where in the track it drifts. Changes from turn 1 are only in the drifting state.

2aThe rule, and Tracks with the tooltip open

Range. Take the lowest and highest 30-second window of crateside's reading, round the lowest down and the highest up. So the range is never a single number, even for a slight drift: 127.6–128.4 reads 127–129.

Spread. (highest − lowest) ÷ the figure shown, using the raw window figures, rounded to a whole percent. Under 1% reads <1%. A 3 BPM swing is 3% at 92 but under 2% at 170. The percentage reflects that, and a range or ±n doesn't.

Wording. drifts 91–94 · 3% · crateside's reading. The same string is the song page note, the tooltip, and (without the source) the Tools card note. On Tracks the flag carries the spread too (2c).

Rapper's Delight is open to show the tooltip. The window figures in 2a–2b are made up. For a warped track, the range and spread come from the warp's segments, not the reading. See the handoff for the open question about short segments.

The flag would read drifts 3% instead of tempo drifts, at the same width. When you're scanning Tracks to decide what to warp next, you can see which drifting tracks are the hard ones without opening each. The spread is a whole percent, never tenths: it's measured from 30-second windows or warp segments, so tenths would claim a precision the measurement doesn't have. Under 1% reads drifts <1%. Brief 13 · issue #92 · ADR 0049 · built against 58a2dae

How a BPM reads, everywhere one appears

Two marks, used the same way everywhere. A bare figure in full weight is a tempo crateside can vouch for: 128 means 128.00. A tilde and a light weight mean approximate: ~123. It is always a whole number, so a tilde never appears next to hundredths. Everything else about a tempo (where it came from, whether it drifts or changes) is said in words: in the note on the song page, in a tooltip, and as a flag beside a title on Tracks. No new tokens or colors.

Song names and BPM figures come from the brief and ADR 0049. Keys, genres and the Dashboard counts are made up for illustration.

1aThe states: one formatter, seven outputs
StateFigureFlag on TracksNote · also the tooltipExampleRule

Two marks for approximate, never one. A tilde and a weight of 400, where a vouched figure is 600. Either one alone tells the two apart, so an amber row (where the color already carries a meaning) or a copied screenshot still reads correctly.

Where it came from uses three phrases everywhere: from your warp, crateside's reading, keytag's estimate. keytag's figure is also drawn at --text-50, because it's the weakest source and only shows up before a reading exists.

Shape is said in words, so the figure keeps its two meanings and nothing more. A drifting tempo is approximate, so it gets the tilde. A mostly-steady or changing tempo shows its main tempo as vouched, because that part is.

Summaries aren't tempos. Bins, peaks and ranges summarize many tracks, so they are whole numbers with no mark. They still go through the same formatter (Range, Bin) so the no-hand-formatting test still covers them.

Re-Pitch uses the same formatter. A shifted figure is calculated from a vouched tempo, so it's vouched too and shows two decimals, or none at 0 when the tempo is whole. The clipboard gets exactly the number on screen, minus any tilde, because After Effects needs a plain number. A drifting track's rows are whole numbers with a tilde. Copying one says what was copied and that it's approximate. The BPM field holds the whole number, with the tilde drawn as a fixed prefix outside the text you can edit. Typing in the field drops the track (as built), and the tilde goes with it. A typed figure counts as vouched. Mostly-steady and changing tracks work from their main tempo as vouched, and the card says part changes tempo / changes tempo. Tools only picks warped songs, so approximate and keytag figures don't show up here.
