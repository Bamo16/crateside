Brief 16 · turn 1 · #96 · ADR 0050

A warp that sits off its tempo, and the button that fixes it

Who owns the problem decides where it shows. Drifts is about the music, so it stays a flag beside the title. Off its tempo is about the warp's figure, so it moves next to the BPM as a small mark (a pennant, the shape the warp view already uses for markers), with the detail on hover. On Track details the song page says outright that your warp is typed at one figure and the audio holds another. The warp view gets an Off the grid row under the tempo line: it plots how far the audio sits from the warp's grid over the track. A warp that is off its tempo draws as a straight slope, a drifting one wanders, and one that steps jumps. True up sits in that row's gutter, beside the tempo, the way Live puts /2 ×2 next to its BPM box. It acts on the track's warp, not on whichever stem is selected.

Figures, offsets and dates are made up. Songs in italics aren't real. The 10 ms line between a filled and a hollow mark is a proposal for the build to check against real tracks.

The mark sits in the invisible .00. An approximate figure is always whole, so the BPM column already holds three empty characters to its right. The pennant goes there: no new width, the digits stay aligned, and it reads as part of the figure rather than the title. Hover the cell for the full note.

Two levels, not a number. Filled: 10 ms or more by the end, worth truing up. Hollow: measurably off, but under 10 ms. That answers "how bad" at a glance. The exact amount lives in the tooltip and in the filter.

An Off its tempo filter beside Tempo drifts gives the amount its own columns and sorts by the drift. The later batch action belongs at this filter's head.

No mark for steps in its grid or grid not confirmed. The ~ already says approximate. The pennant means one thing crateside can fix for you, so it isn't spent on the others.

The off its tempo chip beside the title goes, along with its spacing bug. drifts N% stays where it is.

The row belongs to the track's warp, like the tempo line above it, so True up sits in its gutter and not in a stem's detail. That settles the maintainer's point: selecting a different lane doesn't change what it acts on. The stem detail keeps its two controls.

The drawing is one dot per 10 s window: how far the audio's transients sit from the warp's grid lines there, against a dashed zero line, ±30 ms. A warp that is off its tempo draws a straight slope, so you can see the fix will be a retype.

The status line spans the plot under the row, in the side stack's status shape. The confirm opens in its place, with an amber label and a .danger button, and says what moves and by how much. Writing is teal. Done keeps the row, now flat, so you can see the fix worked.

Not offered: no button, and the status line says why at --text-40, as the other two controls do.

The row draws whenever the check ran, that is, on any steady warp. A warp that holds draws flat with holds, and gets no button or status line. For a warp that changes tempo it isn't drawn.

This answers the brief's last two questions. The three unconfirmed notes get their marks in the drawing, not the text: a slope, a jump, or no dots at all. Off its tempo and drifts look different because they are different shapes, and each sits where its owner is (the BPM for the warp, the title for the music). The milliseconds are drawn here only, never on the stem lanes, which stay Live's clip view.
